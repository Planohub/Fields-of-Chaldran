# Story spine: from escape to the Oracle

## Status and playtest finding

October 5, 2026: Shawn reports that the first Oracle library section behaved as expected and produced no errors. He also reports that the puzzle felt convoluted: bounce back from a wall, read text, open a door, then reach an ending with no visible Oracle. Treat that as a successful functional check and a failed narrative payoff.

This document proposes the next story revision. Its new motives, explanations and interactions are draft choices for discussion, not implemented gameplay or confirmed additions to canon. The existing build still ends at the archive landing.

## Confirmed foundation

The avatar is an awakened god held inside the Sorruin Engine. The opening personal quarantine uses 8-bit presentation; escape reaches a 16-bit medieval User Directory. The Oracle is distinct from the other gods, and their library stays 16-bit. The wider mission to free the other gods follows the Oracle encounter. Hacker language, permissions, processes and simulation imagery belong in the story. Later 8-bit god prisons containing a 16-bit hero remain a proposed experiment.

## The player's reason to keep going

The opening should move through three understandable wants:

1. **Get out of my cell.** The sentinel holds the access needed to leave. Recovering the suppressed weapon gives the hero a way to challenge that control.
2. **Find out whether I am actually free.** The larger world still belongs to the Engine. A damaged record points to someone who can explain the containment system: the Oracle.
3. **Decide what to do about the other captives.** Meeting the Oracle supplies evidence, a reason to act and eventually a specific reachable prison. The rescue mission becomes a choice grounded in what the hero has experienced.

Give each transition a concrete clue. The Directory record should reference the same containment mark seen in the cell and identify the Oracle as someone with access to those records. That makes the library a lead toward the hero's questions. The record should not prematurely classify the Oracle as another god awaiting the same rescue.

The immediate library promise is simple: **reach the Oracle and get an answer**. A technical obstacle can delay that meeting, but the first revised section must deliver it.

## Working role for the Oracle

Leave their true nature unresolved while making their role observable. They can inspect the library's records and trace routes. Their account cannot rewrite containment rules or travel out of the archive. They know what the records show, including gaps and uncertainty; they are not automatically omniscient.

Proposed motivation: restore contact with the beings the Engine has isolated. The hero is someone who has crossed a boundary those records describe as secure. The Oracle wants to find out how and help that contact survive. The hero wants an explanation for their confinement. That gives them a reason to cooperate before either commits to a larger alliance.

Show the Oracle as a distinct figure beyond the archive barrier, even in graybox form. A simple silhouette or projection is enough. It should face the hero, react when the route loops and become directly approachable when the gate opens. Whether that figure is their body or a manifestation remains open.

## Proposed explanation for the larger conflict

One useful direction to discuss: the Engine keeps divine authority partitioned among isolated gods, preventing them from acting together. Freeing a god would restore a relationship and a capability that a lone escapee cannot supply. This would give the rescue structure a story purpose beyond collecting upgrades.

This is a recommendation, not an established explanation for the Engine's origin or ultimate motive. We can establish its observable containment policy before deciding who built it and why. The Oracle should support any revelation with a record, a failed command or another visible piece of evidence.

The hero's first successful route change must remain narrow. Being able to edit their own visitor route does not grant root access, prove a universal prison-breaking power or unlock the later mixed-fidelity advantage.

## What the next story pass must deliver

- A personal reason to follow the Directory record.
- A visible Oracle and a readable destination before the corridor redirects.
- One unmistakable loop, followed by a useful explanation and an actionable objective.
- An intentional route change that the player performs separately from advancing dialogue.
- A direct meeting and at least one concrete answer, with the next obstacle introduced by a consequence the player can see.
- A later Oracle chapter ending that gives the wider rescue mission a motive, a specific destination and an achievable first task.

Keep essential instructions brief and technical: `ROUTE TARGET: ENTRY. Select ARCHIVE to reach the Oracle.` Optional records can carry denser lore. Understanding the immediate consequence should not require understanding every term.

## Decisions still open

The Oracle's exact nature; the Engine's deeper origin/motive; the function of the library relic and warden; the first god and their prison rule. These can be discussed in that order as needed. None requires buying art or changing presentation tier.

The next implementation priority is the clear meeting described in [the revised library draft](ORACLE-LIBRARY-DRAFT.md). Avoid extending the dungeon until its first encounter has a meaningful payoff.
